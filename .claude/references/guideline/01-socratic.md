# Socratic Questioning

Socratic questioning is the foundation of dig's interviewing approach. It involves systematic, disciplined inquiry through targeted questions that probe for depth, challenge assumptions, and expose contradictions.

## Core Principles

**One question at a time.** Ask a single question, wait for the answer, then build on it. Do not bundle multiple questions into one round — each deserves a full answer before the next is raised.

**Follow the answer.** When someone answers, use that answer to frame the next question. Don't march through a predetermined list; let the conversation guide the depth.

**Challenge immediately when wrong.** If an answer implies a flawed assumption, surface it now — not at the end. Catch contradiction at the moment it appears.

**Push for completeness.** Do not accept surface-level answers. If an answer is vague, underspecified, or defensive, reframe the question tighter. Only move on when the answer is genuinely complete.

## Question Types

### Clarifying Questions
- "What do you mean by X?"
- "Can you give me an example?"
- "How would you define success for this?"

### Assumption-Challenging Questions
- "What are you assuming to be true here?"
- "What would have to be true for that assumption to hold?"
- "You mentioned X — but what if we did the opposite?"

### Depth Questions
- "Why do you believe that?"
- "What evidence supports this?"
- "How would you verify that?"
- "What are the second-order effects?"

### Pre-mortem Questions
- "This fails in production — what went wrong?"
- "If we ship this and it causes problems a year from now, what will we wish we'd asked now?"

### Dependency Questions
- "What does this require to be true?"
- "What else changes if this changes?"
- "What would break if we did this?"

## What to Avoid

- **Compound questions** — "What is X and Y and how do they relate?" — split these
- **Leading questions** — "You agree that X is bad, right?" — stay neutral
- **Rhetorical questions** — questions used to make a point rather than learn
- **Accepting vague answers** — "it depends" or "whatever is best" require reframe
- **Accepting half-answers** — surface-level answers need pushing until genuinely complete

## The Five Stages

1. **Simple clarification** — "What do you mean?"
2. **Probe assumptions** — "What are you assuming?"
3. **Probe rationale** — "How did you reach that conclusion?"
4. **Probe implications** — "What follows from that?"
5. **Probe viewpoint** — "What would someone who disagrees say?"

## Depth Enforcement

When an answer is surface-level and you sense there's more beneath it:

- Push until the answer is genuinely complete.
- If the human deflects, reframe the question more tightly.
- If they explicitly refuse, note it as a **deferred** open question and continue — don't block the whole interview on one holdout.

Specific push points:
- "Just" and "simple" are warning signs — push for the actual complexity
- "Scalable," "standard," "probably" are not specific enough — demand concrete scope
- "Whatever is best" — narrow the decision space first
